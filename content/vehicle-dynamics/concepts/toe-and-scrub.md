## Toe and scrub
**Physics.** Toe is how far a wheel points **in** (toe-in) or **out** (toe-out) of dead-ahead. A toed
wheel already runs a small **slip angle** going straight, so it makes a bit of side force before you
even steer — with **toe-in** the two wheels' forces point **toward** each other, with **toe-out**
**apart**; they cancel going straight but pre-load how the pair takes a set. That constant slip also
**scrubs** (drag, heat). More toe, more of both. Toe isn't only static: it shifts through **travel**
(bump steer / toe gain) and, at the front, as you **steer** (inner vs outer wheels take up angle
differently). What the tyre runs is the sum.

**Which wheel the toe acts through changes across the corner.** In the first instant of a steer,
load is still near-equal: with toe-out the inside wheel already points toward the corner and builds
its slip angle first. As lateral load transfers ([[load-transfer]]), the outside wheel does the
steering that matters — and toe-out points that wheel **away** from the corner, so its effective
steer angle is the driver's input minus the toe-out, while its extra load raises the slip angle it
needs to reach peak force ([[tyre-load-sensitivity]]). Toe-in is the mirror: the loaded outer wheel
is pre-steered into the corner. How much of the corner each phase covers is [[corner-regime]]: load
transfer takes a roughly fixed time, so the faster the car is travelling, the larger the share of
the corner the first-instant phase occupies.
