## Bump compliance
**Physics.** A tyre grips most consistently when its **vertical load** stays **steady**, and a rough
surface throws that load up and down. What decides how much of that reaches the tyre is how freely the
wheel can move **vertically relative to the chassis**. Free movement absorbs the input — the wheel
takes it, less reaches the chassis, load stays steadier. Resisted movement passes it through — load
spikes and drops, and the tyre skips or goes **light** (no load, no grip). Steadier load, more grip
([[tyre-load-sensitivity]]).

So bump compliance is set by everything that movement has to fight on its way from tyre to chassis:
what resists the wheel rising, what ties it to the other wheel so a single-wheel input has to move
both, what routes load through the links instead of the springs, and what gives instead of the
suspension.

**The input is not over when the bump is.** A wheel that has moved has to come back, and until it
settles the load is still varying — so how freely the wheel moves and how its return is controlled
are both part of the same answer ([[damping]]).
