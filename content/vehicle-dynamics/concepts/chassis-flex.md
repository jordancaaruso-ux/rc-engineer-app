## Chassis flex
**Physics.** The chassis itself **twists and bends** under cornering and one-wheel (diagonal) loads —
it's a **spring in series with the suspension's roll stiffness** ([[roll-stiffness]]). The more it
flexes, the more of the roll it takes up itself, **softening the effective roll stiffness** and blurring
how cleanly the front/rear split tunes; it also adds **compliance** ([[bump-compliance]]) — the whole
platform gives over bumps and uneven surfaces. Front vs rear flex biases which end is softened.

**The window (founder, 2026-08-28).** How much flex is right moves with grip, and normally the
higher the grip, the stiffer the chassis. **Too much flex for the grip:** the cornering loads are
big enough that the platform gives before the tyre does — the car moves under you and ends up
somewhere other than where it was put; imprecise. **Too little for the grip:** the tyre gives
before anything on the car does — it lets go over bumps and on a low-grip surface with nothing
absorbing it. So more flex is the low-grip direction, and on a high-grip day a car that is hard to
catch is not normally asking for more of it — a stiffer chassis is the usual move there, and the
"too abrupt" problem is answered by the timing levers ([[grip-curve]]), not by softening the
platform.

**Moved by:** chassis material, top decks, brace screws, deck cuts.
