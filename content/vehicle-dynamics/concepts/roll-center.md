## Roll centre
**Physics.** The roll centre (RC) is the geometric point the sprung mass rolls about in a corner,
set by the suspension-linkage pickup points (arm and link angles). Its **height** sets four things:
- **How much load transfers the fast, geometric way** — the higher the RC, the larger the fraction of
  lateral load that reaches the outside tyres through the links (near-instant) rather than through the
  springs (slow). It sets the fast fraction in [[load-transfer]].
- **How much transfers at that end at all** — the link-borne share scales **directly with RC height**
  (that end's mass share × RC height ÷ track × cornering g). Raising one end's RC puts **more** of the
  total transfer on that end, so its tyre pair loads more unevenly ([[tyre-load-sensitivity]]);
  lowering it puts less.
- **Support** — that geometric path is kinematic, so the load it carries **scales with cornering
  force** (springs and ARBs don't scale the same way). A higher RC holds the car up more the harder
  it's driven — support that grows with grip and pace.
- **Jacking** — with the RC **above** ground, cornering load pushes the chassis *upward* in roll, and
  the higher the RC the more it jacks; with the RC **below** ground the sign flips and cornering load
  pulls that end **down** instead.

The gap between CoG height and RC height is the roll lever — the taller it is, the more the car leans
on the springs (the slow path), and the more the body **rolls** for a given g.

**Not a static number.** The RC moves with the chassis: as an end compresses, its RC drops roughly
**one-for-one** with the travel on a touring-car double wishbone. Shim settings therefore give the RC
**at ride height** only — an end running deeper in its travel (softer, more loaded, squatting, lower
ride height) is working at a **lower** RC than its sheet figure says.

**Affects:** [[load-transfer]] (sets both the fast/geometric fraction and that end's share of the
amount) → and through those [[bite-hold]] and [[tyre-load-sensitivity]]; [[on-in-track]] (height sets
that feel directly).
**Moved by:** upper-link geometry (inner + outer shims), inner-lower-arm & under-hub shims — the knobs
that move the pickup points; and ride height / suspension travel [[ride-height-and-rake]], which moves
it without touching a shim.
