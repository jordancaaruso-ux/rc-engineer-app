## Roll centre
**Physics.** The roll centre (RC) is the geometric point the sprung mass rolls about in a corner,
fixed by the suspension-linkage pickup points (arm and link angles). Its **height** sets three things:
- **How much load transfers the fast, geometric way** — the higher the RC, the larger the fraction of
  lateral load that reaches the outside tyres through the links (near-instant) rather than through the
  springs (slow). It sets the fast fraction in [[load-transfer]].
- **Support** — that geometric path is kinematic, so the load it carries **scales with cornering
  force** (springs and ARBs don't scale the same way). A higher RC holds the car up more the harder
  it's driven — support that grows with grip and pace.
- **Jacking** — with a raised RC, cornering load pushes the chassis *upward* in roll; the higher the
  RC, the more the car jacks up. Enough of it can lift or trip the car when overdriven.

The gap between CoG height and RC height is the roll lever — the taller it is, the more the car leans
on the springs (the slow path).

**Affects:** [[load-transfer]] (sets the fast/geometric fraction) → and through it [[bite-hold]];
[[on-in-track]] (height sets that feel directly).
**Moved by:** upper-link geometry (inner + outer shims), inner-lower-arm & under-hub shims — the knobs
that move the pickup points.
