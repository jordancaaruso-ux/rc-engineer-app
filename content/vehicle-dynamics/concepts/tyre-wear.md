## Tyre wear
**Physics.** Use changes the rubber itself, not just how much is left. Working a tyre alters its
structure and that costs grip. Additive taken up by the rubber moves grip the other way over the same
period, so a used tyre is not simply a lower-grip tyre — two changes run at once and which leads is
not derivable from the mileage.

**Wear is unequal, and that is what moves balance.** A tyre wears at the rate it is worked, so the
end doing more work wears faster and loses its grip first — the car drifts away from that end as the
tyres age. Which end that is depends on layout, grip and how the car is driven; the front carries the
steering slip, which is work the rear is not doing.

Unlike heat, wear does not reset between runs. Behaviour that moves across runs with nothing touched
on the car has a cause here.
