## Rear bodyshell height (HRB)

**Keys:** `rear_hrb_setting` — shims that **raise the rear of the bodyshell** on its posts.

**Raising the rear of the shell adds rear grip / stability at speed** (founder-confirmed). The shell is the car's main aero device: lifting its rear changes how air works over and off the body, loading the rear more as speed rises.

The defining property of every bodyshell lever: **effects scale with speed squared.** Strong in fast corners, high-speed direction changes, and long straights into fast entries; close to nothing in slow corners. That makes body height a **phase- and corner-speed-specific** tool:

- Car nervous or loose **only in the fast sections** → rear body height up is a direct, cheap lever that does not disturb slow-corner balance.
- Car pushing in fast corners but fine in slow ones → the same logic in reverse (lower rear shell frees the rear at speed) — hedged; verify.
- A slow-corner handling problem is **not** an aero problem — do not reach for body settings to fix hairpin behaviour.

## Related body parameters

**Keys:** `bodyshell_upstop_height`, `body_position_from_windshield` — body mounting position parameters recorded on the sheet. Fore/aft body position shifts where the shell's aero load acts (further forward biases the front, further back the rear) — directionally reasonable but not founder-verified; treat as theory and defer to what the driver has actually tested. Body **choice** itself (shell model) is a large aero decision outside chassis-tuning scope.
