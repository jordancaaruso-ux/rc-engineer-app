## Tyre working range (temperature, and how hard the tyre is worked)
**Concepts:** [[bite-hold]], [[tyre-load-sensitivity]], [[tyre-wear]], [[load-transfer]], [[roll-stiffness]], [[chassis-flex]]

*(AI draft, 2026-08-28, from the founder's prompt: "stiffening things works the tyre more and can
increase the peak grip of the tyre." Not yet founder-reviewed.)*

**Physics.** Rubber grip depends on its **temperature**. From cold, grip rises as the tyre warms
into a **working range**, holds across it, and falls again past it as the rubber overheats. Where
that range sits is a property of the compound and additive; whether the tyre is *in* it is set by
how much **work** it is doing. The **peak grip** the tyre can give is therefore not fixed for the
day — a tyre below its range has a lower peak than the same tyre inside it.

**Heat comes from work.** A tyre is heated by two things, both of which the setup controls:
- **Slip** — the rubber sliding against the surface as it makes force ([[toe-and-scrub]]). More slip
  angle, more scrub, more heat.
- **Load cycling** — the rubber being compressed and released as vertical load moves on and off it
  through the corner and over bumps ([[load-transfer]]). The faster and harder load arrives, the
  more the carcass is flexed per corner.

So a platform that loads the tyre **sooner and harder** — stiffer springs or bars, a higher roll
centre, a stiffer chassis ([[roll-stiffness]], [[chassis-flex]]) — puts more energy into the rubber
per lap than one that loads it gently and late. The tyre is **worked more**. A softer, slower
platform works it less.

**What that does to grip is set by which side of the range the tyre is on.** Below its range — a
cold day, a green or low-grip surface, a tyre that never comes in — working it harder raises its
temperature toward the range and **raises its peak**: a stiffness change here can *add* grip, not
only move when it arrives or which end carries it. Inside the range, working it harder changes
timing and share only, as [[bite-hold]] and [[corner-regime]] describe, and the peak is what it is.
Past the range — a hot day, a high-grip surface, a tyre already overworked — working it harder
pushes it further over and **lowers** the peak, and the softer, gentler platform is the one that
finds grip. The same stiffness change can therefore add grip on one day and take it away on
another with nothing on the car different; which side of the range the tyre is on is not derivable
from the change alone.

**Two things that look alike.** A car that is short of bite and a car whose tyres are below their
range both read as "no grip" — the difference is that the under-worked tyre **improves through the
run** as it warms, and improves faster the harder it is driven, while a bite shortage is the same
on lap one and lap ten. Whether it comes in through the run is the fact that separates them, and
they can also both be true at once: a soft platform that arrives late *and* never warms the tyre.

**Where this sits against the other low-grip lever.** [[chassis-flex]] gives grip on a low-grip
surface by letting the platform give before the tyre does — compliance. Working the tyre harder
gives grip by raising the tyre's own peak. They pull the stiffness the opposite way, and both are
real; which is the one that helps today is where the tyre sits against its range, which the run
tells you and the change does not.

**Resets between runs.** Heat is gone by the next run; wear is not ([[tyre-wear]]). Behaviour that
is different at the end of a run from the start is heat; behaviour that is different between runs
with nothing touched is wear.
