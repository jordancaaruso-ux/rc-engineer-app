## Toe on the grip curve

**Keys:** `toe_rear`, `toe_front`, `toe_gain_shims_rear`

**Toe starts a tyre partway up its curve.** A toed wheel runs a slip angle before the car turns ([[toe-and-scrub]]), so its tyre is already partway up its grip curve ([[grip-curve]]) when the corner starts. In a corner, rear toe-in adds to the outer rear tyre's slip and takes from the inner's, so the outer tyre — the loaded one ([[loaded-wheel]]) — reaches its peak at a smaller body angle. `unverified`

**Each step buys less.** The curve flattens as it nears its peak. The same step of toe added to a tyre already high on its curve adds less grip than it did lower down, while the scrub — drag on every straight, heat in the tyre — grows with every step. `unverified`

**Past a point it costs grip at the limit.** Toed, the two tyres at one end reach their peaks at different body angles: the outer sooner, the inner later. Near the limit one is past its peak while the other is short of it, so the most grip that end can make together falls as the toe grows. Where that point is depends on the tyre, the day's grip and the toe the car gains as it rolls ([[bump-steer-toe-gain]]); nothing here gives it as a number. `unverified — not derivable from the angle alone`
