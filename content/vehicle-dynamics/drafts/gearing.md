## Gearing (final drive ratio)

**Keys:** `spur`, `pinion`, `fdr`, `motor`, `motor_timing`

**What it is.** The final drive ratio (FDR) is how many times the motor turns for one turn of the wheels: spur teeth ÷ pinion teeth × the car's internal ratio — the fixed reduction in its belts or gears, printed in the car's manual. A bigger pinion or a smaller spur lowers the FDR number; a smaller pinion or a bigger spur raises it. `unverified` (definition)

**Rollout** is how far the car travels for one turn of the motor: tyre circumference ÷ FDR. Tyre diameter is part of the gearing — a smaller tyre does what a higher FDR number does. `unverified` (definition)

## What the mechanism pushes

**A higher FDR number** (shorter gearing): the motor turns more for every metre, so the same motor torque is a bigger push at the tyre, and the motor reaches its top revs at a lower car speed. `unverified`

**A lower FDR number** (taller gearing): a smaller push at the tyre for the same motor torque, and top revs come at a higher car speed. `unverified`

**Heat.** An electric motor's torque comes from current, and its heating rises with the square of the current. Taller gearing asks the motor for more torque to give the same acceleration, and holds it at low revs — where it draws the most current — for longer out of every corner. On the same track, driven the same way, taller gearing runs the motor and the speed controller hotter and shorter gearing runs them cooler. `unverified`

**A motor wind does not give a ratio.** Where the motor's revs run out depends on the length of the longest straight and the speed the car carries onto it. How hot it runs depends on the layout, the timing on the motor, the battery, the tyre diameter and the air temperature. The same motor is geared differently on a short tight track and on a long open one. `unverified — not derivable from the motor alone`

## What the driver can see

- **On the longest straight.** The car stops gaining speed well before the braking point: the motor has run out of revs on that straight. Taller gearing — a bigger pinion or a smaller spur, a lower FDR number — raises the speed at which the revs run out; shorter gearing lowers it. `unverified`
- **Still gaining speed at the braking point:** the motor has not run out of revs, so its revs are not what holds the car's speed down on that straight. Which way to gear that car is not settled by anything on this page: low in its revs a motor pushes harder through a shorter gear, high in its revs through a taller one, and the driver cannot see which of the two the motor is in. `unverified`
- **Motor temperature straight after a run,** against the limit the motor's maker gives. A motor at its limit is taken past it by taller gearing.
- **What other cars run with the same motor on the same track.** That ratio already has that track's straight and that motor in it, which no figure worked out away from the track has. `unverified — the founder's own starting method is owed here`
