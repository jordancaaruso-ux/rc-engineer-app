## Droop and downstop (extension travel)
**Keys:** `droop_front`, `droop_rear`, `downstop_front`, `downstop_rear`
**Concepts:** [[bump-compliance]], [[load-transfer]], [[corner-regime]]

**Physics.** Droop/downstop set how far a wheel can **extend** before it hits its **limit**. More
extension lets the wheel reach down to keep **contact** over bumps and as the car rolls; run out of it
and the wheel **tops out** — it can't extend further, so from there that corner goes rigid in droop and
load transfers through a stiff path. Sheets differ on whether a lower number means more or less travel —
read the column label.

**Topping out is a switch, and droop sets when it comes.** Whichever wheel unloads extends. Before
the limit that corner takes load slowly through its springs; past it the path is rigid and load
arrives at once. More extension delays the switch, so load builds later there — the
[[corner-regime]] timing mechanism. Past the limit the end is stiffer in roll too, so its
steady-state share moves.
