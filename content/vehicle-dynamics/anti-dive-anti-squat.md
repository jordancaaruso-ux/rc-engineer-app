## Anti-dive and anti-squat (side-view geometry)
**Keys:** `under_lower_arm_shims_ff`, `under_lower_arm_shims_fr`, `under_lower_arm_shims_rf`, `under_lower_arm_shims_rr`
**Concepts:** [[load-transfer]], [[bite-hold]], [[bump-compliance]]

**Physics.** The **split** between the forward and rearward inner pickups on an axle (FF−FR front, RF−RR
rear) tilts the lower arm's **inner hinge line in side view**, setting a **pitch centre** — the fore-aft
twin of the [[roll-center]]. It decides how much **longitudinal** load (braking front, drive rear) reacts
through the **links** instead of the springs: more anti → **less pitch** (dive/squat) and **faster**
fore-aft transfer ([[load-transfer]] → [[bite-hold]]). The settled total is unchanged (CoG height +
wheelbase). Trade: force fought through the links also fights [[bump-compliance]] under brakes/power.
Geometry, on any car: raising the **rear arm's forward** pickup = more anti-squat; raising the
**front arm's rearward** pickup = more anti-dive. Which way a given car's shims go is the car's
business. (The **averaged** stack height is separate — roll-centre support, see [[under-lower-arm]].)
