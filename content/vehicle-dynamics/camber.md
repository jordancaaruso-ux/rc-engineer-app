## Camber
**Keys:** `camber_front`, `camber_rear`
**Concepts:** [[camber-grip]], [[camber-gain]]

**Physics.** Camber is the **tilt** of the tyre relative to the road. More **negative** camber tilts the
top of the tyre inward, helping the **outside** tyre stay nearer flat as the car rolls. The tyre runs
its **static** camber plus [[camber-gain]] in roll; what that effective tilt does to grip is [[camber-grip]].

**Where the number sits** (founder, 2026-09-09). The tyre sets the camber it wants ([[camber-grip]]),
and it says so across its tread: even wear or heat across the tyre is a patch working flat at full
roll; an inside edge worn more is too much negative, an outside edge too little. On a touring car
that number is near 2°, and the car stays there. A camber move is the move when the tread says the
number is off. Past the number, more camber does not trade a little of one thing for another: the
patch shrinks and grip goes everywhere the tyre works — the middle, braking and the straight. So a
car whose tread wears even has its camber, and a balance complaint is answered by the levers that
move balance; camber is reached for last, not first.
