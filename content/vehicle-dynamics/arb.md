## Anti-roll bar (ARB / sway bar)
**Keys:** `arb_front`, `arb_rear`
**Concepts:** [[roll-stiffness]], [[corner-regime]], [[bite-hold]], [[load-transfer]], [[tyre-load-sensitivity]], [[bump-compliance]]

**Physics.** An ARB is a **bar linking the left and right wheels** at one end of the car. It only
twists when the two wheels move **differently** — that is, in **roll** — so a stiffer bar resists roll
more at that end. When both wheels move **together** (**heave** — a crest, or a bump under both) the bar
just swings and does nothing. So it changes that end's share of roll stiffness without touching ride
height or heave.

## Anti-roll bar — which way it answers, by corner

A bar change drives the same two mechanisms a spring does, pulling **opposite directions**, decided
by the corner rather than by the bar ([[corner-regime]]).

**In a corner the car settles into** — a long, fast sweeper — each axle's share of the lateral load
transfer is set by its roll stiffness and its roll centre height, and the end carrying the larger
share gives up side grip ([[tyre-load-sensitivity]]). Stiffer at an end takes the larger share
there; softer takes the smaller.

**In a corner the car never settles into** — a hairpin, a chicane, a flick, and every corner entry —
the stiffer end takes load, and builds grip, **sooner**; the softer end builds it **later**
([[bite-hold]]). Stiffer at an end brings its grip in earlier; softer brings it in later.

The two do not add up to one direction: a stiffer rear frees the rear in a sweeper the car settles
into but plants it in a hairpin or flick; a softer rear supports the rear in a corner the car
settles into but lets it yaw in a quick change of direction. Answer for the corner in front of you,
and say which one you answered for.

**What a bar changes that a spring does not.** A bar moves roll stiffness **only**. A spring of the
same effect also changes how that end carries a bump under both wheels and how deep it rides in its
travel. So the bar is the narrower tool when only the roll balance needs moving; the spring reaches
both when the end is riding too deep or too high as well.

**What makes the change bigger or smaller.**

- **How much transfer there is to move.** The settled mechanism can only redistribute the transfer
  that exists, so it shrinks as cornering force falls — with grip, and with speed ([[corner-regime]]).
- **How hard that tyre is already worked.** It bites harder the further the loaded tyre already
  sits into the falling part of its curve ([[tyre-load-sensitivity]]).
- **Nothing at all before the car has rolled**, and nothing when both wheels rise together
  ([[load-transfer]], [[bump-compliance]]).
